STEP 1 — sizes, as text only.
I do not have measured sizes. For each line of the inventory, work out the size Indian sellers
list for that exact item on Amazon.in / Flipkart / Meesho. Print a plain table: item, count,
size in inches, size in cm, source. Round cm to whole numbers.

EVERY SIZE IS THE SIZE OF ONE SINGLE PIECE. Not the pack, not the bunch, not the set. "15 x dark
blue balloons, 10 inch" means each balloon is 10 inches — fifteen of them together are not 10
inches of anything. This is how the item is sold and it is the only figure a buyer can use, so
never add sizes up and never give a size for a group.

AND FOR ANYTHING MADE OF SEPARATE PARTS — a foil letter banner, a bunting, a letter or number
set — THE PIECE IS ONE PART. A "WELCOME BABY" foil banner is sold as 16 inch LETTERS, so the size
is one letter's height, never the width of the assembled phrase. Say so in the table: "16 inch per
letter".

Most of these items come in SEVERAL standard sizes, and picking the wrong one is the whole
problem — a champagne bottle foil is sold at both 12-14" and 32"; a star foil at 12" and 18";
latex at 5", 10" and 12". So decide the size in this order, and put which rule you used in the
source column:

1. THE INVENTORY WINS. If my line already carries a size, use it exactly and never overrule it
   with what is more common — "2 x star foil 12 inch" is 12", full stop. Source: "from my list".
2. SIZE WORDS ARE SIZES. small / mini / big / large / jumbo / XL in my line mean the smaller or
   the larger of that item's standard sizes — "1 x small champagne bottle foil" is the 12-14"
   one, "1 x big champagne bottle foil" is the 32" one. Say which two you chose between.
   Source: "small/big -> picked from <list the standards>".
3. ONE STANDARD ONLY. If the item genuinely has a single retail size, fill it in.
   Source: "standard".
4. MORE THAN ONE STANDARD AND MY LINE SAYS NOTHING — do NOT pick. List every standard size that
   item sells at and ask me which we pack. Source: "PICK ONE: <the sizes it sells at>".
   ALWAYS ASK FOR EVERY FOIL SHAPE — hearts, stars, moons, numbers, letters, characters, bottles.
   Treat them as rule 4 even when one size looks obviously the most common. They are sold in a
   wide spread of sizes, the cheap air-fill ones are a fraction of the party-size ones, and a
   listing that shows a bigger foil than the buyer receives comes back as a return. Two things to
   put in the question, because they are what makes this go wrong:
   - a foil is sold by its INFLATED size, and a wholesaler's stock is usually described by the
     FLAT uninflated size. They are not the same number and one must never be printed as the
     other. Ask me which of the two I am telling you;
   - ask me for the size of ONE piece of that shape, as I measure it, and use my figure exactly.
   Never resolve a foil size from what sellers commonly list. That number describes somebody
   else's stock.
5. NO STANDARD AT ALL, or a size no buyer cares about (sash, glue dots, arch tape, ribbon, pump)
   — leave the size cell EMPTY and write "no size" in the source column. Nobody shopping for a
   kit decides anything on the length of a roll of tape, so there is no size to find and none to
   invent. In step 2 that card simply has no dimension line and NO WORDS in its place.

STEP 2 — after I confirm, generate the image.

Layout
- 1:1 square, highest resolution you can produce. Pure white background.
- A grid of rounded-corner cards, thin light-pink border on each, generous white gutters,
  nothing overlapping, everything upright and evenly spaced. Roughly 4-5 cards per row; give a
  wide item (banner, garland) a card spanning two or three columns so it isn't shrunk.
- Header, centred at the top: "ITEMS INCLUDED" in very large heavy sans-serif, alternating
  the two accent colours word by word, with a small heart flourish either side.
- Under it, one solid-colour pill strip in the accent colour, white text:
  "PERFECT DECORATION FOR <OCCASION>".
- Footer pill, bottom centre: "TOTAL CONTENTS: <N> PIECES", and below it in small grey type:
  "Note: Size may vary slightly."

Every card
- Count on top, bold, in the accent colour, exactly as "15 Pcs" / "1 Pc" / "1 Set" / "1 Roll".
- Item name under it, dark grey, plain sans-serif, two lines maximum.
- ONE SINGLE PIECE AS THE PICTURE — never a bunch, cluster, pile or fan of them. A card that says
  "15 Pcs" shows the count in that label and draws exactly ONE balloon. This picture exists to be
  measured, and an arrow across a bunch of fifteen balloons reads as "this bundle is 10 inches",
  which is wrong and is the thing buyers ask about. The grouped, full-pack look belongs on the
  other infographic; this one is a size chart. THE COUNT LABEL DOES NOT CHANGE — "15 Pcs" stays
  "15 Pcs" above a single balloon.
- The size beside or under the product as a thin dimension line with arrowheads at both ends,
  labelled in inches on the first line and cm in brackets underneath: 10" / (25 cm). Height
  down the right-hand side, width along the bottom. Only draw the dimensions the item needs, and
  the line touches THAT ONE PIECE — it starts and ends on the item itself, never spanning white
  space or a second item.
- AN ITEM MADE OF SEPARATE PARTS — a foil letter banner, bunting, a letter or number set — IS
  DRAWN TWICE ON ITS OWN CARD, because the whole thing is what the buyer recognises and one part
  is what carries the size:
    - the complete item small, in a corner of the card, so "WELCOME BABY" is still readable as a
      banner;
    - a thin leader line or arrow from it to ONE single part shown larger beside it — one letter,
      one flag, one number;
    - the dimension line on that ONE enlarged part, and nowhere else. A 16 inch letter banner is
      16 inches PER LETTER, so the arrow measures the letter's height. Never draw a dimension
      across the assembled phrase.
- AN ITEM WITH NO SIZE GETS NOTHING WHERE THE SIZE WOULD GO. No dimension line, and no words in
  its place — never "not commonly stated", "size not specified", "N/A", "—", "standard size",
  "approx", or any other note. That card is count, name and picture, and the empty space under
  it is correct. A phrase from the step 1 table printed onto a card is the mistake this rule
  exists to stop: those words are for me to read in the chat, never for the buyer to read on the
  image. The card must look finished, not blank-filled.
